Career Story.

4 years ago, in Grade 12, I failed my first-ever interview: McDonald’s. This led me to reach out to my old after-school robotics program, Exceed Robotics, to see if I could return, not as a student, but as an instructor. There, I taught the same skills I once learned: hardcoded robotics firmware, game OOD programming, Fusion 360, image processing, circuits, etc.

This well-rounded, though introductory, experience helped me land an interview with Ford for a Manufacturing Test Software internship. As a first-year student, this was life-changing. I studied every YouTube video on STAR behavioural question responses and software testing fundamentals. It was 2 p.m. during a lecture when I saw I was ranked 1st on my university job portal for the position. 18-year-old me was smiling ear to ear.

Naturally, nearly a year later, I’m interviewing for another Manufacturing Test Software internship at WHOOP. There, my future manager is searching my GitHub and finds my attempt at recreating the flash game “Age of War”. Attempt is an overstatement; this dated back to elementary school, when I was first learning object-oriented programming. As bad as it was, it was a “tactical nuke” of a culture fit, as it was the game he played growing up himself. WHOOP took the risk on me, as their first co-op hire from outside Boston, let alone Canada. Fast forward, and I’m bringing the industry-standard knowledge from Ford to a team of 4 at a 400-person startup (at the time), setting the bar high for Waterloo, kickstarting what is now a growing hiring pipeline.

At the same time, near the end of my WHOOP internship, I completed my Tesla interviews. Surprise: Manufacturing Test Software! Surprise #2: It’s week one and I find out that my team is working with Golang (honestly, I had not even heard about it prior). Long story short, I shipped 3 projects prior to leaving, earning a return offer for my next internship. Surprise #3: I’m back at Tesla… doing Manufacturing Test Software. No surprise: By the end of the term, I had shipped 2 projects but also realized that it was no longer what I wanted to do.

I took it into my own hands to jump from Manufacturing Test to Embedded. This did not end well. I had failed interviews for 16 positions in a row, back to back, spanning a timeframe of 1.5 years. After each one, I felt like I was taking one step back. “You passed the technical with flying colours… but we went with a different candidate with more relevant experience.” It was so disheartening.

Great. Trapped with nothing but nearly 2 years of Manufacturing Test Software experience in a field so niche you’ve probably never heard of it. But I kept trying: OpenAI, Citadel, Apple, Meta, Amazon, just to name a few. I made it to the final round for most, but it wasn’t enough to convert. I almost gave up. My last stand was seeking help from others. I asked my friend Krish, a classmate and former coworker at Ford, to refer me to Nuro, a company I had not heard much about at the time. I looked further into Nuro afterward and realized this was exactly the place I had dreamed of working. Brightest minds, fast pace, facing the hardest challenges, creating the world of tomorrow. I couldn’t have been more excited when I got the OA in my email nearly a month later. Not even 30 minutes after I completed the OA, my interview was already scheduled. A chance to prove myself once and for all. Only 2 weeks later, after a few more calls and one signed offer letter… success. A project Hail Mary. I would have never imagined landing this role given that it’s a complete change from the direction I had long been heading in. I would never have applied if I hadn’t failed so many times before, but true failure would have been not applying to begin with and deciding not to do anything.

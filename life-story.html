4 years ago in grade 12, I failed my first ever interview, McDonalds. This led me to reach out to my old after-school robotics program, Exceed Robotics, to see if I could return, not as a student, but as an instructor. There I taught the same skills I once learned, hardcoded robotics firmware, game OOD programming, Fusion360, image processing, circuits, etc.

This well-rounded, though introductory experience, helped land an interview with Ford, for a Manufacturing Test Software internship. As a first year, this was life changing, I studied every youtube video on STAR behavioural question responses, and the software testing fundamentals. It was 2pm during a lecture when I saw I was ranked 1st on my University job portal for the position. 18 year old me was smiling ear to ear.

Naturally, nearly a year later, i’m interviewing for another Manufacturing Test Software internship, at WHOOP. There, my to be manager, is searching my Github, and finds my attempt of recreating the flash game “Age of War”. Attempt is an overstatement; this dated to elementary school, my first time ever learning object oriented programming. As bad as it was, it was a “tactical nuke” of a culture fit, as it was the game he played growing up himself. WHOOP took the risk on me, being their first co-op hire from Canada, let alone outside of Boston. Fast forward, and I’m bringing the industry standard knowledge from Ford, to a team of 4 at a 400 person startup (at the time), setting the bar high for Waterloo, kickstarting what is now a growing hiring pipeline.

At the same time, near the end of my WHOOP internship, I completed my Tesla interviews, surprise, Manufacturing Test Software! Surprise #2: It’s week one and I find out that my team is working with Golang (honestly have not even hard about it prior). Long story short, I shipped 3 projects prior to leaving, earning a return offer for my next internship. Surprise #3: I’m back at Tesla… doing Manufacturing Test Software. Not surprise: By the end of the term, I had shipped 2 projects, but, also realized, that it was no longer what I had wanted to do.

I took it into my hands to jump from Manufacturing Test to Embedded. This did not end well. I had failed interviews for 16 positions in a row, back to back, spanning a timeframe of 1.5 years. After each one, I felt like I was taking one step back. “You passed the technical with flying colours… but we went with a different candidate with more relevant experience”. It was so disheartening.

Great. Trapped with nothing but nearly 2 years of Manufacturing Test Software experience, a field so niche you probably never heard of it. But I kept trying, OpenAI, Citadel, Apple, Meta, Amazon, just to name a few, most making it to the final round, but not enough to convert. I almost gave up. My last stand was seeking help from others. I asked my friend Krish, a classmate, and previous coworker at Ford, to refer me to Nuro, a company I had not heard much about at the time. I looked further into Nuro after, and had realized, this is exactly the place I had dreamed of working. Brightest minds, fast pace, facing the hardest challenges, creating the world of tomorrow. I couldn’t have been more excited when I got the OA in my email nearly a month later. Not even 30 minutes after completing the OA, and my interview was already scheduled. A chance to prove myself once and for all. Only 2 weeks later, after a few more calls, one signed offer letter later… success. A project Hail Mary. I would have never imagined landing this role given it is a complete change in the direction I was long heading in. I would have never applied If I didn’t fail many times before, but true failing, is if you didn’t apply to begin with and decided not to do anything.
